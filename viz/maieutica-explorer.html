Draw out what your organisation already knows.

Strategy sits on top, skills at the bottom, and projects and people are the two-way bridge between them. Everything on this page is stored as four simple link tables (goal–project, project–person, person–skill, project–skill). Hover any node to light up its neighbourhood; click to pin it and read the details.

Three networks, all computed from the stored links: who works with whom, which use cases are entangled (shared people and skills), and which skills live in the same heads. Switch layers on and off, view them separately or overlapped, and read every node's betweenness (who sits on the paths between others) and structural holes (gaps only this node bridges).

What does each use case cost (people, time), how complex is it (duration, number of skill sets, depth), and what is it worth? Value is risk-adjusted (expected value × confidence). Bubbles below the red line return less than 1.25× their cost in a year: under-optimised.

Pick someone who is leaving. Maieutica reads what they actually did (skills in use, projects, collaborators), shows what breaks, ranks internal successors, and drafts a job description from real work, not from a stale one.

A new colleague joins. Instead of a form, Maieutica chats: it reads their CV or asks a few targeted questions, proposes skills with evidence, and nothing enters the graph until the person confirms it. It then asks only what the organisation is actually missing. Try it: the sample CV is editable.
